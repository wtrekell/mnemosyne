# What research organizations say UX portfolios need (March to October 2026)

## Findings

### Scope and the date rule

This report uses only sources published on or after 2026-03-01 that carry a date. That rule removes several of the most-cited 2026 sources, so the evidence base is thinner than a casual search suggests. Excluded by date: NN/g's "State of UX 2026" (16 January 2026), NN/g's "UX Hiring: Insights from a Design Recruiter" (16 January 2026), Figma's design hiring study and its blog post "Why demand for designers is on the rise" (10 February 2026), Figma's "State of the Designer 2026" (12 February 2026), the UXPA salary survey (latest edition 2024), BLS Employment Projections 2024 to 2034 (August 2025), and the UX Design Institute "State of UX Hiring" report (2024). Excluded as undated: UXfolio's "State of UX Portfolios 2026" (the only portfolio-specific survey of hiring decision-makers found; its page shows only a 2026 copyright), the User Interviews and Levels.fyi 2026 UX Salary Report, and an undated blog that relays BLS OEWS and Indeed Hiring Lab figures. None of their figures are used below.

No research organization published a dated, portfolio-specific study inside the window. What the window contains is evidence on skills employers weigh, how designers' work is changing, and posting volumes. Portfolio implications are drawn from that evidence and labeled as inference.

### What portfolios need to show

**AI fluency is the top-rising hiring criterion, but craft has not lost weight.** In Designer Fund and Foundation Capital's AI in Design 2026 report, 50% of surveyed design leaders said they place greater emphasis on AI fluency when hiring, with systems thinking and strategic skills close behind [S1] [C2]. A secondary summary of the same report gives the leaders' order as AI proficiency, then systems thinking, strategy, and narrative or storytelling skills, with 22% ranking technical and coding skills among the top criteria [S2] [C2]. Only 5% of leaders said they place less emphasis on execution quality, and leaders and designers both said taste, judgment and a high standard matter more now that AI makes "good enough" output easy [S1] [S2] [C3]. The report's own summary pairs AI fluency with "a high bar for craft, vision, and storytelling" [S1] [C3].

**Judgment about what to build is the skill the large surveys emphasize.** Figma's 2026 AI report (8,403 survey responses and 639 interviews across designers, developers and PMs) argues that when AI makes prototyping, copy and assets cheap, deciding what ships becomes the differentiator; 90% of respondents say design is at least as important as before AI [S3] [C7]. It does not discuss hiring or portfolios.

**Specific AI work counts for more than being "AI-adjacent."** NN/g's Sarah Gibbons describes four AI design orientations: designing with AI, designing AI products, designing for AI agents, and designing the AI itself (model behavior, evaluation criteria). She argues that demand for agent-facing and model-behavior work is outrunning the supply of qualified designers, and advises naming the specific kind of AI work rather than calling oneself an "AI designer" [S4] [C11]. The article states no method or data; it is an expert framing, not a study.

**Building and shipping code is spreading into design roles.** Across Designer Fund's survey, 65% of designers say they take on more product or engineering work and 50% say they have shipped AI-generated code to production [S1] [C5]. Figma reports that the share of designers doing development work rose from 21% to 41% [S3] [C6]. UX Tools' Spring 2026 survey (1,478 designers and builders, fielded 14 March to 6 April 2026, recruited through its own newsletter and sponsors) found 43.8% spend more than half their building time on AI-generated code, and the top planned investments for the next 12 months are AI-assisted coding (64.0%), agent workflows (46.3%) and design systems (40.2%) [S6] [C8]. Three independent surveys agree on the direction, though none is a random sample [S1] [S3] [S6] [C9].

**Synthesis for portfolios (inference, not a measured finding).** Read together, these sources point to portfolios that show (a) the designer's judgment and trade-offs, not only finished screens; (b) concrete, named AI work, including how AI was used and where its output was rejected; (c) evidence of building or shipping, such as working prototypes or code; and (d) craft held to a high standard [S1] [S3] [S4] [S6] [C24]. No source in the window measured how hiring managers score portfolios against these elements, so their relative weight is unknown. A UX Design Institute blog (a training provider) gives matching advice to show reasoning, research behind decisions and impact on the product, without data [S14] [C23].

### AI skills in postings

Adobe's June 2026 study of US creative job postings (about 10,500 postings in September 2025 and 11,300 in April 2026, the second wave collected by Revelio Labs) found postings rose about 8%, and the share explicitly requiring AI skills rose from 10% to 15%. About 85% still do not require AI explicitly [S7] [C12]. Mid-market and enterprise postings required AI more often (18%) than solo and small-business postings (8%) [S7] [C12]. The report lists UX/UI Designer at 8% in its role breakdown of AI-requiring postings; whether that is the role's share of AI-requiring postings or the share of UX/UI postings that require AI is unclear from the page, and the collection method changed between waves [S7] [C13].

Autodesk's 2026 AI Jobs Report (13 July 2026) lists AI UX Designer as the fastest-growing AI title in its Design and Make industries (+145%) and design skills as the top in-demand skill for AI roles; the page names no data source or sample [S8] [C14].

NACE's Spring 2026 employer survey (185 respondents, fielded 12 February to 17 March 2026) reports that employers say "more than one-third of entry-level jobs require AI skills," nearly triple the fall 2025 figure [S9] [C15]. This is all occupations, not design, and the question-level base is not stated; secondary readers put the base at a subset of employers who are increasing hiring [S9] [C15]. No dated source in the window gives a UX-specific seniority mix or entry-level share.

Taken together, employers increasingly ask for AI skills, but explicit AI requirements remain a minority of design postings [S1] [S7] [S8] [S9] [C10].

### State of the UX job market

**Demand signals are mixed.** Adobe's posting count rose about 8% over six months [S7] [C12]. Academy UX's June 2026 update, built on Adzuna posting counts, shows the opposite month to month: 52,670 US postings across its categories, with Product Design at 16,410 (down 9.9%), UX Research at 922 (down 18.1%), Design Engineering at 2,512 (down 16.7%) and Design Operations up 33.5%; its unexplained "Heat Score" fell from 68 to 40 [S10] [C16]. A secondary summary of Designer Fund's report says 60% of design leaders expect to maintain or grow headcount [S2] [C20]. These measure different things over different periods, so they do not settle the direction of demand [S2] [S7] [S10] [C19].

**Companies have not caught up with the changed role.** Only 28% of design leaders in Designer Fund's survey say their companies have formally updated evaluation, compensation or hiring; 8% changed performance metrics [S1] [C4]. Weekly AI use for design work rose from 54% to 91% and daily use is 75% [S1] [C1].

**Broader design employment.** The UK Design Council's Design Economy 2026 (published 7 September 2026) reports design employment up 15% from 2020 to 2025 and 2.27 million design roles, but the council concedes its sizing predates widespread generative AI, and it does not separate UX or digital design [S11] [S12] [C17].

**Field label.** In an NN/g survey of 604 practitioners (April 2026, non-representative), "UX" appeared in 47.8% of responses naming the field, while product designers were more likely than UX practitioners to say "product" (27% versus 10%) [S5] [C18].

### Statistics that cannot be traced to a primary study

- **"78% of recruiters use AI to screen portfolios."** The earliest dated source found is Muzli's portfolio guide (31 March 2026), which states it without naming a survey, organization or method [S13] [C21]. It is widely repeated in 2026 portfolio guides. The excluded UXfolio survey reports a figure in the opposite direction, so this number should not be used.
- **"82% of design leaders say their need for designers stayed the same or grew."** It is repeated in-window, for example by the UX Design Institute (20 March 2026) [S14] [C22], but its primary source is Figma's design hiring study of 10 February 2026, which falls outside the date rule and whose full method is gated.
- Other circulating figures (a 2.3x interview lift for portfolios with measurable outcomes, "500 to 800 applicants per entry-level posting," "nearly 80% of portfolios parsed by AI") turned up only in vendor or career blogs with no traceable study, and are not used.

### Consensus versus single-source claims

- **Consensus (three or more independent sources):** design roles are expanding into building and code [C9]; AI skills are increasingly expected [C10].
- **Single-source:** craft and execution keep their weight while AI fluency rises (Designer Fund only) [C3]; the four AI design orientations and the supply gap for agent and model-behavior work (NN/g only, no data) [C11]; leader headcount expectations (secondary summary only) [C20]; posting declines in June 2026 (Academy UX only) [C16].

## Conflicts

**Is UX demand rising or falling in 2026?** Adobe [S7] (Unconfirmed) reports US creative postings up about 8% from September 2025 to April 2026. Academy UX [S10] (Unconfirmed) reports June 2026 month-over-month declines in Product Design, UX Research and Design Engineering postings. A secondary summary of Designer Fund's report [S2] (Unconfirmed) says 60% of design leaders expect to hold or grow headcount. The datasets, categories and periods differ (Adobe covers all creative roles over six months; Academy UX covers one month of Adzuna counts). Effect: the report states demand as mixed and draws no trend conclusion [C19].

**Do recruiters use AI to screen portfolios?** Muzli [S13] (Unconfirmed) asserts 78% do, with no study behind it. The only survey found that addresses it, UXfolio's, is excluded as undated. Effect: no in-window finding on AI screening; the 78% figure is flagged as untraceable [C21].

## Appendix A: Next searches

1. Get the full UXfolio "State of UX Portfolios 2026" report and establish its release date; if it is on or after 2026-03-01, it is the only direct survey of what hiring decision-makers want in portfolios (case-study structure, outcomes, AI disclosure, red flags).
2. Pull BLS OEWS May 2025 national figures for SOC 15-1255 (Web and Digital Interface Designers) and 15-1255-adjacent occupations directly from bls.gov (released spring 2026), and check Indeed Hiring Lab's own 2026 publications for design-adjacent posting indexes.
3. Read the Designer Fund "Teams" chapter of AI in Design 2026 for its leader sample size, ranked hiring criteria with percentages, and junior versus senior hiring data.
4. Look for 2026 UX-specific seniority and entry-level posting data from Revelio Labs, Lightcast or LinkedIn Economic Graph.
5. Check whether UXPA, MeasuringU or AIGA field a 2026 salary or hiring survey later in 2026.
